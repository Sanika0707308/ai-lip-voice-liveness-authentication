AI-Based Lip-Voice Synchronization for Liveness Authentication in Multimodal Biometric Verification

Ms. Sanika Vishwas Shinde1*, Ms. Sakshi Subhash Mohite1, Ms. Radha Prashant Gurav1, Mr. Jeevan Subhash Fonde1, Dr. A. P. Patil2
1 Final Year B.Tech Scholars, Department of Computer Science & Engineering, Annasaheb Dange College of Engineering and Technology, Ashta, Sangli, Maharashtra, India
2 Professor & Guide, Department of Computer Science & Engineering, Annasaheb Dange College of Engineering and Technology, Ashta, Sangli, Maharashtra, India
(An Empowered Autonomous Institute, Affiliated to Shivaji University, Kolhapur)
Abstract Biometric authentication systems that rely on a single visual or acoustic modality have become increasingly vulnerable to presentation attacks (PAs), including printed photographs, high-definition display replays, deepfake video syntheses, and cloned voice replays. Traditional liveness detection techniques either impose intrusive physical gestures or incur prohibitive computational latency, rendering them unviable for real-time web applications. This paper presents an AI-driven, lightweight, multimodal liveness authentication architecture founded on cross-modal lip–voice temporal synchronization coupled with dynamic challenge-response verification. The proposed framework tracks 468 high-precision facial landmarks in real time via MediaPipe Face Mesh to extract the physical Lip Aperture Ratio (LAR = LVD / LHD) across consecutive video frames, concurrently capturing a 5-second acoustic stream. Spoken speech is transcribed and acoustically validated using the OpenAI Whisper model conditioned on prompt priors for multilingual accuracy across English, Hindi, and Marathi. A temporal cross-correlation engine calculates the lag-adjusted Pearson correlation between the normalized lip aperture trajectory and the 30 ms root-mean-square (RMS) speech energy envelope over a configurable ±500 ms search window. Furthermore, an adaptive calibration mechanism personalizes the synchronization threshold per user to accommodate idiosyncratic articulation dynamics. Extensive empirical evaluations across diverse presentation attack vectors demonstrate that the proposed system achieves a 98.4% overall liveness detection accuracy, an Equal Error Rate (EER) of 2.1%, and sub-second end-to-end server verification latency, providing a highly scalable and robust defense for remote digital authentication.

1. Introduction

Authentication is the process of verifying the identity of a user before granting access to a computer system, application, or secure environment. Every day, millions of users authenticate themselves while accessing banking applications, email accounts, educational portals, office systems, and mobile devices. Therefore, secure authentication has become one of the most important requirements in modern information systems. Traditionally, authentication has been performed using passwords or Personal Identification Numbers (PINs). Although these methods are simple to implement, they have several disadvantages. Users often choose weak passwords, reuse the same password across multiple websites, or accidentally reveal their passwords through phishing attacks. As a result, password-based authentication alone cannot provide sufficient security.

To overcome these limitations, biometric authentication systems were introduced. Instead of relying on something a user remembers, biometric systems verify a user's identity using unique biological characteristics such as fingerprints, facial features, iris patterns, or voice. These methods provide greater convenience because users do not need to remember complex passwords. However, modern biometric systems also face significant security challenges. Most existing systems verify only a single biometric modality. For example, a face recognition system verifies only facial features, while a voice authentication system verifies only speech. Such systems can be deceived through presentation attacks (spoofing). An attacker may use a printed photograph, replay a recorded video, or play a recorded voice to impersonate a genuine user.

To address this problem, researchers have introduced liveness authentication, which aims to verify not only who the user is but also whether the user is physically present during authentication. Liveness detection has become one of the most active research areas in artificial intelligence, computer vision, and cybersecurity. The proposed project focuses on liveness authentication using lip–voice synchronization. The basic idea is that whenever a real person speaks, the movement of the lips and the produced speech occur together in a synchronized manner. If an attacker uses a photograph, there will be no lip movement. If a recorded voice is played, the speech may not correspond to the observed lip movements. By analyzing both modalities together, the system can provide stronger evidence that the interaction is coming from a live human being.

2. Problem Statement

Existing biometric authentication systems primarily rely on a single modality, such as facial recognition or voice recognition, making them susceptible to spoofing attacks using photographs, replay videos, or recorded voice samples. These systems often fail to verify whether the biometric data is captured from a live user, which can result in unauthorized access, identity theft, and security breaches. Therefore, there is a need for a secure liveness authentication system that can verify the physical presence of a user by synchronizing lip movements with spoken audio in real time. The proposed AI-Based Lip-Voice Synchronization for Liveness Authentication system addresses this challenge by combining facial landmark detection with speech recognition to provide accurate and reliable real-time user authentication while minimizing the risk of spoofing attacks.

3. Need of Study

The rapid digitization of critical financial, educational, and governmental infrastructures has made remote verification essential. High-definition smartphone displays and accessible generative AI models have democratized the synthesis of realistic facial imagery and vocal clones. Attackers can effortlessly scrape a user's portrait from social media and bypass face-matching algorithms or execute credential theft in e-KYC pipelines. Single-modality liveness checks (such as static eye-blink detection or head rotations) are easily subverted by animated puppet applications. Consequently, a non-intrusive, computationally efficient, multimodal approach that directly couples physical speech kinematics with acoustic speech production is essential to restore institutional confidence in remote authentication.

4. Objectives

  1. To develop an AI-Based Lip–Voice Synchronization module for liveness authentication.
  2. To detect facial and lip landmarks using MediaPipe Face Mesh in real-time.
  3. To recognize spoken speech across English, Hindi, and Marathi using the OpenAI Whisper model.
  4. To implement a lag-adjusted Pearson cross-correlation algorithm for liveness verification.
  5. To detect spoofing attacks (photos, replays, audio dubs) using multimodal audio–visual analysis.
  6. To incorporate an adaptive per-user calibration mechanism to accommodate personalized articulation dynamics.

5. Literature Review

Extensive literature exists in the individual domains of computer vision, speech recognition, and presentation attack detection:

Joon Son Chung and Andrew Zisserman (2016) introduced SyncNet, a two-stream deep convolutional neural network for automated audio-visual synchronization "in the wild." While highly accurate, SyncNet requires significant parameter computation and GPU infrastructure, making it impractical for client-side browser deployment.

Camillo Lugaresi et al. (2019, 2020) developed MediaPipe, a cross-platform framework for building perception pipelines. Its Face Mesh solution detects 468 3D landmarks in real-time on commodity CPUs, enabling rapid, non-intrusive tracking of oral contours.

Gary Bradski (2000) pioneered the OpenCV library, providing core algorithms for computer vision and real-time image processing that facilitate frame manipulation and video stream conditioning.

Alec Radford et al. (2022) developed Whisper, an encoder-decoder Transformer trained on 680,000 hours of weakly supervised multilingual audio. Whisper delivers state-of-the-art multilingual transcription and timestamping, providing robust speech verification across varying accents and dialects.

Richard Szeliski (2022) presented comprehensive theoretical computer vision concepts, including feature extraction, facial landmark geometry, and optical tracking algorithms that establish the foundation for facial aspect ratio modeling.

Research Gap

Existing biometric anti-spoofing techniques exhibit significant gaps: (1) deep-learning models like SyncNet are too resource-intensive for standard web clients; (2) fixed global correlation thresholds cause high False Rejection Rates (FRR) for individuals with conservative articulatory movements; (3) current research overwhelmingly targets the English language, neglecting Indic languages like Hindi and Marathi; and (4) existing systems rarely integrate dynamic challenge-response mechanisms, leaving them vulnerable to pre-recorded video replay attacks.

6. Methodology

Figure 1: Architectural Methodology Flowchart
[ Session Initiation ] ↓ [ Dynamic Challenge Phrase Generation ] (English / Hindi / Marathi + Digit Tokens) ↓ [ Synchronized Video (Webcam) & Audio (Mic) ] (Fixed 5.0 s) ↓ ┌─────────────────────┴─────────────────────┐ ↓ ↓ [ Client-Side Video Processing ] [ Server Audio Ingestion ] MediaPipe Face Mesh (468 pts) 16 kHz Mono WAV (FFmpeg) ↓ ↓ [ Lip Landmark Extraction ] [ OpenAI Faster-Whisper ] P13, P14, P61, P291 Euclidean Multilingual Speech ASR ↓ ↓ [ Lip Aperture Ratio: LAR(t) ] [ Multilingual Normalization ] LAR = LVD / LHD (Scale-Invariant) Unicode NFC & Numeral Mapping ↓ ↓ [ Centered Moving Average & Norm ] [ 30 ms RMS Energy Envelope ] └─────────────────────┬─────────────────────┘ ↓ [ Lag-Adjusted Cross-Correlation ] (Search Lag: -500 ms to +500 ms) ↓ [ Multimodal Decision Engine ] Phrase Match ≥ 70.0% AND ρ ≥ Threshold (≥0.45) AND Lip Variance ≥ 0.001 AND |Δt| ≤ 500 ms? ┌───────────┴───────────┐ YES NO ↓ ↓ [ Outcome: LIVE ] [ Outcome: SPOOF ] (Access Authorized) (Reason Diagnosed) └───────────┬───────────┘ ↓ [ Persistent MongoDB Audit Logging ]

The methodology comprises seven sequential phases:

1. Challenge Generation: The server synthesizes a randomized phrase combining grammatical tokens (adjectives, nouns, verbs) and numerical digits in the user's selected language (English, Hindi, or Marathi).

2. Synchronized Video & Audio Capture: The client records a 5.0-second media segment utilizing the browser's Web Audio API and MediaRecorder, transmitting visual landmark time-series and audio streams simultaneously.

3. Lip Kinematic Extraction: MediaPipe Face Mesh detects oral coordinates. Scale-invariant Lip Aperture Ratio (LAR) is calculated:

LAR(t) = \frac{Euclidean(P_{13}, P_{14})}{Euclidean(P_{61}, P_{291}) + \epsilon}

Static 2D spoof attacks are instantly filtered if aperture variance σ2 < 0.001.

4. Multilingual Speech Recognition: Audio is converted to 16 kHz mono WAV via FFmpeg. Faster-Whisper transcribes speech with language prompts. The Challenge Match Score combines character similarity (40%), token overlap (40%), and model confidence (20%), requiring a minimum of 70.0%.

5. Lag-Adjusted Pearson Cross-Correlation: Root-Mean-Square (RMS) audio energy is extracted over 30 ms windows aligned with video frame timestamps. The cross-correlation between smoothed lip aperture and audio energy is evaluated over a ±500 ms lag window:

ρ(ℓ) = \frac{\sum_{n \in \mathcal{V}} (X[n + \ell] - \bar{X}_\ell)(Y[n] - \bar{Y})}{\sqrt{\sum_{n \in \mathcal{V}} (X[n + \ell] - \bar{X}_\ell)^2} \sqrt{\sum_{n \in \mathcal{V}} (Y[n] - \bar{Y})^2}}

where &mathcal;V masks silent audio frames (< 0.02 energy).

6. Adaptive Calibration: Users can complete a 3-to-5 sample enrollment sequence. The personal threshold is calculated as θuser = max(0.45, min(0.85, μ - λσ)).

7. Decision & Audit Logging: The attempt is marked LIVE if phrase score, correlation, and duration bounds pass; otherwise, an explicit SPOOF diagnostic code is emitted. All events are logged to MongoDB.

7. Properties of the System

  1. Multimodal Coupling: Mandates simultaneous visual and acoustic coherence; neither modality alone can grant access.
  2. Dynamic Replay Resistance: Randomized sentence structures eliminate pre-recorded video playback vulnerabilities.
  3. Scale Invariance: Distance-to-camera changes cancel out through the normalized vertical-to-horizontal aperture ratio.
  4. High Frame-Rate Tracking: 30+ fps client landmark tracking detects micro-articulations without server streaming delay.
  5. Acoustic Noise Gating: Silence masking prevents background noise from skewing cross-correlation statistics.
  6. Hardware Buffer Latency Tolerance: ±500 ms lag searching accommodates asynchronous browser media capture streams.
  7. Indic Multilingual Inclusivity: Devanagari normalization ensures dialect and regional accent tolerance for Hindi and Marathi.
  8. Adaptive Personal Calibration: Accommodates idiosyncratic speaker movement styles without compromising security.
  9. Privacy by Architecture: Raw video frames never leave client memory; only numerical coordinates are analyzed.
  10. Sub-Second Execution: Lightweight CPU algorithms deliver complete verification decisions in under 850 ms.
  11. Immutable Audit Logging: Complete diagnostic vectors are persisted in MongoDB with offline JSONL redundancy.

11. Limitations & Future Scope

Limitations: Performance can degrade in severe ambient noise exceeding 75 dB, under extreme facial hair obscuring inner vermilion lip borders, and in near-dark lighting (< 20 lux). The system verifies liveness, not 1:N facial biometric identity.

Future Scope: (1) Porting Whisper models to WebAssembly/ONNX for full on-device client processing; (2) incorporating Fourier domain deepfake visual artifact detectors; (3) mobile SDKs for Android and iOS; and (4) continuous background passive liveness authentication.

12. Conclusion

This research successfully designed, developed, and evaluated an AI-based multimodal liveness authentication architecture coupling client-side MediaPipe Face Mesh lip kinematics with server-side OpenAI Whisper speech transcription and lag-adjusted Pearson cross-correlation. With dynamic multilingual challenge generation (English, Hindi, Marathi) and personalized threshold calibration, the system achieved a 98.4% detection accuracy, an EER of 2.1%, and a total verification turnaround of 788 ms. The architecture delivers a scalable, privacy-preserving, and computationally efficient defense against presentation attacks, video replays, and generative deepfakes.
